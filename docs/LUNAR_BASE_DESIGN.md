# LUNARIS lunar base design

LUNARIS depicts a conceptual outpost on an elevated lunar south-polar site. The scene's sunlight is fixed. The site, layout, and hardware are educational models and require engineering validation before they could inform a real habitat design.

| System | Engineering rationale and NASA reference | LUNARIS representation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habitation and storm shelter | A long-duration surface habitat must address radiation and impact hazards. NASA-hosted studies examine regolith shielding for these conditions. [NASA Technical Reports Server](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240008927/downloads/Seismic%20Assessment%20of%20a%20Long-term%20Lunar%20Habitat.pdf) | Berms and hull bands communicate shielding around the cylindrical habitat. Recessed windows sit beneath protective brows. The windowless shelter has more continuous regolith coverage and a reinforced entrance. Shield shape and thickness are illustrative. |
| Greenhouse | NASA's ISS Advanced Plant Habitat uses LEDs and a controlled chamber. Its documented porous substrate is different from this game's hydroponic beds. On the Moon a chamber must also manage solar heating and exposure. [NASA Advanced Plant Habitat](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/advanced-plant-habitat/) · [NASA lunar south-pole environment](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/moonbase-environment/) | An insulated roof and opaque wall sections replace broad glazing. Small framed viewing panes keep crops and grow-light strips visible. The trays and manifolds suggest recirculating nutrient delivery. |
| Solar power and storage | The polar Sun stays low, and local terrain controls when arrays receive sunlight. Battery storage can bridge interruptions in generation. [NASA lunar south-pole environment](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/moonbase-environment/) | Steep, supported tables face the fixed preview light from the negative X and positive Z direction. Covered conduit and a junction box indicate distribution. The battery racks and radiator indicate separate storage and thermal hardware. The model specifies no output, autonomy, or tracking schedule. |
| Airlock and pressurized access | The ISS Quest airlock separates suit support from entry and exit operations. Pressure boundaries allow crew transfer while retaining the main cabin atmosphere. [NASA Quest Airlock](https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/quest-airlock/) | Inner and outer doors mark the transfer chamber. The exterior cabinet represents EVA support equipment. Ribbed connectors and collars mark pressurized passageway interfaces. Door operation and pressure cycling remain outside the model. |
| Heat rejection | Radiators reject excess heat to space as thermal radiation. Heat transport to the radiator and its exposure to sunlight affect performance. [NASA JPL thermal control](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/nmp/st8/tech/heat_pipe_tech1.php) | Panel assemblies appear on the habitat, greenhouse, and battery module. Their area, orientation, and thermal links are schematic. |

The upgraded geometry uses the existing shared canvas, camera, selection API, and material batching. All default facilities remain within the 140 × 140 m placement zone. The corridors are intentional connections between facilities.

## Visual review

Run npm ci and npm run dev, then open /src/scene/buildings/preview.html on the Vite URL. Inspect both sides of the habitat and shelter entrances, greenhouse crop visibility, solar supports and battery racks. Orbit, zoom, and pan. Select each building and open ground, check shadow continuity, and watch for stutter on a representative student laptop.
