# LAMOON science audit, Levels 1–3

One tick is an Earth-length planning day for the exercise. All numerical inputs in the game are gameplay assumptions. The audit compares physical relationships and units with NASA sources and identifies discrepancies. These levels use the same engine; Level 3 adds two scheduled training faults and retains the existing resource rates.

| Mechanic | Game calculation and meaning | Assessment and NASA basis |

| Oxygen | 36 kg store; 0.85 kg per person per day; up to 4.2 kg/day generated, scaled by condition and supplied generator power. Crew draw is scaled by delivered life-support fraction, and low life-support power still lowers health. | **NASA-supported:** spacecraft can use powered water electrolysis. **Needs correction:** production does not draw from water stocks, and reduced life support reduces modeled oxygen demand. Track electrolysis feed and separate metabolic need from delivered oxygen before treating this as engineering. NASA demonstrated 98% recovery for an ISS system with brine processing; 75% here is a game setting, not an ISS or lunar measurement. [NASA ISS water recovery milestone](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/) |
| Food | 65 kg stock; 0.65 kg per person per day; up to 1.8 kg/day × greenhouse condition × powered fraction. | **NASA-supported:** controlled growing systems can supplement crew food. **Needs correction:** mass is not a nutrition measure and the greenhouse has no modeled water input, germination delay, or harvest cycle. The greenhouse lesson teaches roots need water, while engine yields currently respond to electricity and condition. [NASA Growing Plants in Space](https://www.nasa.gov/exploration-research-and-technology/growing-plants-in-space/) |

Offline equipment consumes no device load. | **NASA-supported:** sunlight can power outposts. **Educational simplification:** constant daily yield, priority order, and linear condition effects. **Needs correction for a site model:** no solar angle, shadow, site illumination or night inputs. Orienting the array in Level 2 repairs condition; orientation is not a persistent simulation variable. Level 2's catalog mentions a radiation event around Day 5, but the played level has no scheduled radiation storm. [NASA Weather on the Moon](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/weather-on-the-moon/) · [NASA Space Weather Database](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) |

Level 3 also gates advancement on required objectives. | **Educational simplification:** consistent points and fractions, but these thresholds, durations and effects are invented. They do not represent medical limits, NASA radiation policy, or distinct crew exposure or needs. The game applies the same loss to all living crew. |
| Wear, failures and repairs | Daily condition losses L1/L2/L3: .002/.003/.004. Ordinary incident chances 0%/5%/8%; L2/L3 incident losses .15/.20. The crew starts with one shared stock of five kits; a repair uses one, restoring up to 1.0 condition in these three training levels. Level 3 reserves the final kit for water. | **Educational simplification:** seeded rolls, thresholds, fast repairs and generic kits are teaching mechanics. ## Official challenge resources used

The [Space Apps challenge Resources tab](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/) lists NASA's Space Weather Database (DONKI) and OSDR RadLab. Level 3 uses the [May 2024 NASA storm record and its DONKI events](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/storms/may2024/) in its **NASA Science · Space weather** panel to explain why real radiation hazards can vary with solar events while the game uses a fixed practice dose. DONKI is a cited teaching reference here; the game does not ingest live space-weather data or calculate crew dose from it. RadLab measurements from ISS instruments would also require context and cannot supply a lunar surface dose directly. [NASA DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) · [NASA OSDR RadLab](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/)

## Proposed corrections and playability impact

- Couple oxygen production to water and separate demand from delivered oxygen. Electrolysis would increase water demand and could make the Day 9 outage more severe. Rebalance water stores only after testing all levels.
- Represent greenhouse irrigation, growth time and dietary contribution. Lower or delayed yield would draw down stores sooner, especially on longer missions.
- Drive solar energy and battery losses with a documented site and an illumination schedule. Lower energy availability would cascade through oxygen, water, and food. Review load budgets and reserve size first.
- Model radiation scenarios separately from routine supply penalties using mission-relevant, sourced dose assumptions. A solar-event exercise would change survival difficulty and should be validated independently.
- Track source water, recovery timing and treatment quality before interpreting clean-water output as potable water from a particular technology.
- Replace generic wear and crew health with calibrated models only if the project needs engineering fidelity. Current numeric thresholds should remain labeled as practice values.

Levels 4 and 5 are catalog entries only. Level 3 completion does not unlock an unimplemented level.
